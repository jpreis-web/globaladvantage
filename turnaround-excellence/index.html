The Risk-First Approach to Turnaround Management guides decisions on timing, scope, preparation, contracting, execution, startup and closeout. Anticipation is not simply a planning virtue: it is the discipline that improves the quality and timing of those decisions.

Success is measured in the right order.

The four turnaround objectives form a hierarchy for decisions and a framework for evaluating outcomes.

01

Safety

Complete the turnaround safely, protecting people, assets and the environment.

02

Quality

Complete the required work to the defined technical and quality standards.

03

Duration

Complete within the approved duration and established timeline.

04

Cost

Complete on or under the approved budget.

Cost and duration are essential. Neither justifies compromising safety or quality.

Questions worth asking

Practical answers to common decisions and concerns.

What does a Risk-First Approach change in practice?

It makes risk assessment the starting point for turnaround timing, scope, readiness, contracting, execution choices and post-turnaround learning rather than a separate reporting exercise.

Does “anticipate” mean making the plan more conservative?

No. Anticipation is about earlier information and better decisions. It can enable leaner scope, more efficient execution and more credible contingencies when the underlying risks are understood.

What are the four objectives of a turnaround?

In strict priority: safety, quality, duration and cost. Meeting schedule or budget targets does not compensate for a safety or quality failure.

Why give contractor productivity so much attention?

Contractor productivity influences work completion rates, resource consumption and turnaround duration. It depends on both contractor capability and owner-provided workfront readiness.
