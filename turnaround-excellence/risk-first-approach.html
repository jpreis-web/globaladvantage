THE GAP TURNAROUND FRAMEWORK

Risk first.
At every decision.

The strategic foundation of GAP Turnaround Excellence: risk is the starting point for decisions across the complete turnaround lifecycle.

FOUNDATIONAL APPROACH

Start with the risk. Then decide.

Risk should inform when to stop, which work to undertake, what can be deferred, how the work will be prepared and who should deliver it.

The purpose is not to turn risk into paperwork. It is to improve the quality of decisions, make trade-offs transparent and connect preventive action to the risks that matter most.

THE SIGNATURE PRINCIPLE

“A turnaround is an exercise into risk management.”

Risk-First Approach to Turnaround Management

GLOBAL ADVANTAGE PARTNERSHIP

Risk at every decision point

The same risk-based reasoning applies from long-range asset strategy to the learning carried into the next turnaround.

01 / WHEN

Turnaround timing

Balance asset integrity, reliability, production and business risks to support the timing decision.

02 / WHAT

Scope selection

Distinguish mandatory obligations from discretionary work. Evaluate inclusions, exclusions and deferrals with explicit risk ownership.

03 / HOW

Execution strategy

Choose sequencing, isolation, contracting, logistics and contingency strategies that reflect actual risk exposure.

04 / READY?

Assurance and gates

Challenge whether required safeguards, workfronts, resources and decision systems are sufficiently mature for progression.

05 / DURING

Dynamic execution control

Recognize changing conditions, escalate material risk and protect safety and quality while managing progress.

06 / AFTER

Closeout and learning

Compare anticipated and actual risks, examine controls and use evidence to improve the next cycle.

01

Identify the decision

Define what needs to be decided and the alternatives available.

02

Understand exposure

Assess consequence, likelihood, uncertainty and applicable mandatory constraints.

03

Choose controls

Select safeguards, resources, timing and contingency appropriate to the exposure.

04

Assign ownership

Record the decision, responsibilities and conditions for revisiting it.

05

Reassess and learn

Monitor changes through delivery and integrate findings into the next cycle.

DECISION DISCIPLINE

A risk register is not a risk-first culture.

Risk registers are useful records. The differentiator is whether risk genuinely changes decisions and receives action before consequences occur.

The approach should operate through scope reviews, readiness gates, management-of-change decisions, independent challenges and field-level response mechanisms.
