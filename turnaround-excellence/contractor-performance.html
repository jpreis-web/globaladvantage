TURNAROUND CONTRACTOR PERFORMANCE

Contractor performance.
Measured where it matters.

The most important drivers of turnaround duration and cost are often found at the workfront: readiness, supervision, actual output and constraints.

CONTRACTOR PERFORMANCE

Productivity is a system outcome.

The resources arriving on site are not the same as productive hours delivered. Turnaround performance depends on how effectively the whole execution system converts available capacity into quality work completed.

Contractor management must address selection, scope definition, productivity assumptions, supervision, field measurement, performance response and the conditions created by the asset owner.

GLOBAL ADVANTAGE PARTNERSHIP

Six levers of contractor performance

An integrated approach avoids confusing additional personnel with improved work completion.

01

Contracting strategy

Match work packaging, commercial models and contractor capability to real execution risk.

02

Selection & capacity

Challenge relevant experience, available resources, management systems and competing commitments.

03

Workfront readiness

Ensure access, permits, isolations, materials, drawings, equipment and logistics are available.

04

Productivity measurement

Track earned progress, direct and indirect labor, planned versus actual output and constraint causes.

05

Field supervision

Clarify workface priorities, coordination, interfaces, escalation and short-interval control.

06

Performance improvement

Intervene early, resolve root causes and use lessons learned in the next contract cycle.

OWNER AND CONTRACTOR ACCOUNTABILITY

Contractors cannot be productive on unavailable workfronts.

Performance measurement must be fair, transparent and actionable. It needs to distinguish contractor execution failures from owner-side blockers.

A robust performance system examines output, workforce utilization, rework, schedule adherence and the actual constraints reducing productive time.

Owner-side enablers

Workfronts, permits, materials, engineering, lifting and access.

Contractor execution

Crews, supervision, sequencing, quality and output rates.

Shared information

Agreed baselines, field measurement and transparent reporting.

Timely intervention

Action owners, escalation thresholds and daily removal of constraints.
